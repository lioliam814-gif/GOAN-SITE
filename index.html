Goa is a coastline.
Also a state of mind.

Past the beach shacks and the party strip, the state folds inward — into spice hills, river islands and forgotten forts. Here are eight places worth the detour.

Eight places to roam next

Numbered to match the map above — start wherever the coast pulls you.

1

Cotigao Wildlife Sanctuary

Far south, near Canacona

Goa's second-largest forest reserve, and its quietest. A watchtower overlooks a stream where gaur and giant squirrels turn up at dusk — no crowds, no vendors, just dense sal forest and birdsong.

2

Cabo de Rama Fort

South Goa headland

A crumbling Portuguese fort on a cliff, said to be named for Rama's exile in the Ramayana. Fewer visitors than the famous forts up north, and the cliffside view over the Arabian Sea more than earns the drive.

3

Netravali & the Bubble Lake

Sanguem taluka

Deep in a wildlife sanctuary sits a small sacred pond where gas bubbles rise from the lakebed on being clapped at — locals treat it as a shrine. Pair it with the sanctuary's waterfalls on the same trip.

4

Tambdi Surla Temple

Sanguem, at the forest's edge

Goa's oldest surviving temple — 13th century basalt, carved by Kadamba-era hands, standing alone against a forest backdrop. Almost nothing else from that period survived Goa's later religious upheavals; this one just quietly did.

5

Divar Island

Mandovi river, off Old Goa

Reachable only by a free flat-bottomed ferry, Divar keeps its own pace — paddy fields, Portuguese-era mansions, and a village feast, the Bonderam, that turns the whole island into a flag-decorated street party each August.

6

Fontainhas Latin Quarter

Panjim

Narrow lanes of ochre, indigo and mustard houses with overhanging balconies — Goa's old Portuguese quarter, still lived-in rather than preserved as a museum piece. Best walked slowly, early, before the day's heat sets in.

7

Chorla Ghat

Sattari, on the Karnataka border

Where the Western Ghats spill into Goa — waterfalls, monsoon mist, and a biodiversity hotspot with far fewer tourists than the beaches two hours west. Come in the rains for the falls, in winter for the cool air and views.

8

Mayem Lake

Bicholim, North Goa's interior

A still, forest-ringed lake far from the coastal circuit — pedal boats, a short nature trail, and a picnic spot locals keep mostly to themselves. Good for a slow half-day between the ghats and the beaches.

Distances and access can change with the monsoon — check local conditions before setting out, especially for the sanctuaries and the ghat roads.
